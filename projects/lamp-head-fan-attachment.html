Fan-Cooled Lamp Head Attachment

As a Mechanical Engineering R&D Intern at Energetiq Technology, a subsidiary of Hamamatsu Photonics, I designed a fan-cooled attachment for an LDLS® lamp head, adapting the thermal model I built and validated in my water-cooled lamp head project. The add-on needed to regulate surface temperature, minimize acoustic output, and address particle and stability concerns.

Context
R&D internship, Energetiq Technology, Wilmington, MA · Summer 2026
My role
Project owner: requirements, simulation setup, fan, placement and funnel studies
Tools
SolidWorks Flow Simulation, SolidWorks
Status
Studies underway when the internship ended

Product details, specifications, data, results and project images are left out to protect Energetiq Technology's proprietary information. I'm happy to discuss the approach in more depth.

Project Justification

  • Project management requested a fan-cooled add-on for a lamp head.
  • Existing thermal-management guidance was not specific.

Project Requirements

  • Keep lamp-head surface temperature below a specified maximum.
  • Define optimal fan placement, including standoff distance and orientation.
  • Minimize acoustic noise.
  • Direct airflow away from the lamp head due to particle and stability concerns.

Simulation and Testing

  • Adapted the validated lamp-head thermal model from the water-cooled project in SolidWorks Flow Simulation as the analysis basis.
  • Reconfigured the model for forced-air convection to evaluate the fan add-on.
  • Compared multiple fans across CFM ratings and diameters to balance cooling against acoustic noise.
  • Investigated fan standoff distance to determine optimal placement.
  • Compared custom intake air funnel geometries in SolidWorks Flow Simulation, channeling intake air across the lamp head and into the fan.
  • Evaluated fan selection, placement and ducting together, since increased airflow improves cooling but also increases acoustic noise and affects where air is directed.

Status

  • Fan, placement and funnel studies were underway at the end of the internship, built on a thermal model already validated against empirical data.
